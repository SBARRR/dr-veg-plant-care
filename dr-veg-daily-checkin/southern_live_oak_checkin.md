Southern Live Oak

Today, keep the soil evenly moist but not soggy since the oak is still recovering from transplant shock and root damage. Avoid fertilizing or pruning for now, and keep it in its current bright, airy outdoor spot to support steady recovery.

Top priority/action for today: Check soil moisture by feeling the top 2–3 inches and pot weight; water deeply only if the soil feels dry to the touch and the pot feels light.

Watering guidance: When watering, soak thoroughly until water drains freely from the bottom, then let the pot drain completely—never let it sit in standing water.

One useful check: Inspect the leaves for any new orange spots or browning and note if the new growth looks healthy or stressed.

Optional care improvement: If you want, gently brush off any visible powdery residue on leaves with a soft cloth to reduce fungal spores without spraying.

Optional practical idea: Set a simple reminder to check soil moisture every 3–4 days to avoid over- or under-watering during this recovery phase.
